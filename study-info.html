Light_NDD Research Study

Study Information

Light sensitivity, sleep, circadian rhythms, and wellbeing
in neurodevelopmental conditions

Overview

Many autistic and ADHD people have difficulties with sleep and with their body clock (circadian rhythms), but we don’t yet fully understand why.

The research study Light_NDD explores whether sensitivity to light plays a role in sleep, circadian rhythms and wellbeing in people aged 18–25 who are autistic, have ADHD, or both (AuDHD), compared with people without a neurodevelopmental condition.

We measure how the eyes respond to light, everyday light exposure, sleep patterns and wellbeing.

We hope to improve understanding of the role of light in neurodevelopmental conditions, raise awareness of lived experiences of light sensitivity, and help inform future light-based support.

Aims

Aim 01

Investigate how the pupils of the eyes respond to light in adults aged 18-25 who are autistic, have ADHD, or both (AuDHD), compared with adults without a neurodevelopmental condition.

Aim 02

Explore how everyday light exposure, sleep and wellbeing are connected, using both wearable sensors and self-reports experiences.

Aim 03

Involve people with lived experience in shaping the study.
Our Lived Experience Advisory Group, made up of autistic and ADHD people, will advise us at every stage.

Raising awareness

Light sensitivity is a common experience among neurodivergent people, but it is rarely talked about.
Through the initiative My Light, My Night, we invite people to share photographs that show what light sensitivity is like for them.
